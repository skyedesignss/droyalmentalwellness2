Privacy Policy

Your privacy and confidentiality matter. This page explains how we collect, use, and protect information.

Effective date: September 28, 2026

Last updated: September 28, 2026

This Notice describes how medical information about you may be used and disclosed and how you can get access to this information. Please review it carefully.

1. Who this Notice covers

Droyal Mental Wellness (“we,” “us,” or “our”) provides outpatient mental health services and psychiatric rehabilitation in Reisterstown, Maryland. This page is our website Privacy Policy and our Notice of Privacy Practices under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA).

It applies to information you submit through this website, including the appointment request form and the intake form, and to protected health information created or received when you become a client.

You will also be offered a copy of this Notice at or before your first service visit. You may request a paper copy at any time.

2. Information we collect

We collect only what we need to respond to you, deliver care, and operate this website.

  • Contact details: name, phone number, email address, and mailing address when you call us, email us, request an appointment, or complete intake.
  • Appointment and intake information: the reason you are seeking care, services of interest, scheduling preferences, insurance details you choose to share, and other information entered on our forms.
  • Health and treatment information: once you are a client, records related to evaluation, therapy, medication management, rehabilitation services, billing, and care coordination. This is protected health information under HIPAA.
  • Website technical data: IP address, browser type, device information, and pages visited, collected automatically to keep the site working and secure.
  • Communications: messages you send by form, email, or phone.

Submitting an appointment or intake form does not by itself create a treatment relationship. We use that information to review your request and follow up. Clinical records begin when services are accepted and care starts.

3. How we use and share information

We do not sell your personal information or your health information. We use and disclose information for the purposes below.

Treatment

We may use and share information to evaluate your needs, provide therapy or rehabilitation services, coordinate care with other professionals involved in your treatment, and follow up on appointment or intake requests.

Payment

We may use and share information to confirm insurance coverage, submit claims, obtain payment, and discuss billing with you or your plan.

Health care operations

We may use and share information to run our practice, train staff, improve quality, maintain records, and contact you about your request or services.

Website and form administration

We use website and form data to respond to requests, keep the site secure, and improve how forms work.

Other uses allowed or required by law

We may also disclose information when:

  • you give written authorization;
  • the law requires it, including court orders, certain public health reports, and mandatory reporting;
  • it is needed to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat to health or safety;
  • it is necessary for our legal defense or to protect the rights and safety of our staff, clients, or others, as permitted by law.

Mental health records receive additional protection under Maryland law. Where Maryland law is stricter than HIPAA, we follow the stricter rule. We do not share information for marketing, and we do not sell health information. Most uses of psychotherapy notes, if we maintain them, require your written authorization.

4. Website forms, cookies, and third-party tools

Our appointment and intake forms collect personal information so we can respond to you. Those submissions are treated as confidential and, once they become part of a clinical record, as protected health information.

The site may use cookies or similar tools for basic function, security, and limited usage insight. You can control cookies in your browser. Turning some cookies off may affect how the site works.

Pages may link to third-party sites, such as video platforms or mapping tools. Their privacy practices are their own. Review those policies before you share information there.

5. How we protect information

We use administrative, technical, and physical safeguards designed to limit access to information to people who need it for their work. No electronic transmission or storage method is completely secure. We cannot promise absolute security, but we work to reduce risk and to notify you if the law requires notice after a breach of unsecured protected health information.

6. Children and families

This website is not intended for children under 13 to use on their own. When we serve minors, we collect and handle information under applicable law, with parent or guardian involvement as required. Maryland law also addresses when a minor may consent to certain outpatient mental health services. Ask us if you need that explained for your situation.

7. Your rights

You have rights regarding your information. Subject to legal limits, you may:

  • ask to see or receive a copy of your records;
  • ask us to correct information you believe is wrong or incomplete;
  • ask us to limit certain uses or disclosures, though we may not always be able to agree;
  • ask us to contact you in a specific way or at a specific address;
  • ask for a list of certain disclosures we have made;
  • receive a paper copy of this Notice;
  • revoke a written authorization, except to the extent we have already relied on it.

To use these rights, contact us using the information in Section 10. We may ask you to put the request in writing.

8. Our duties

We are required by law to:

  • maintain the privacy and security of protected health information;
  • give you this Notice of our legal duties and privacy practices;
  • follow the Notice that is currently in effect;
  • notify you if a breach of unsecured protected health information occurs, as required by law.

We will not retaliate against you for filing a privacy complaint or exercising your rights.

9. Complaints

If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you may contact Droyal Mental Wellness using the details below. You may also file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Civil Rights:

Online: hhs.gov/hipaa/filing-a-complaint
By mail or phone: Office for Civil Rights, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services

Filing a complaint will not change the care we offer you.
